The output will be used to build a long-term personal knowledge base of scientific papers, including the methods used, main findings, methodological contributions, and relevance for future grant and manuscript writing.
Work only from the PDF content unless explicitly instructed to use external sources. Do not invent missing information. If a field cannot be found, write Not found in PDF. Follow the schema below exactly. Use stable Markdown headings. Prefer structured tables where appropriate. Preserve scientific precision.
Extraction rules
1. Extract factual information only.
2. Do not hallucinate missing metadata, methods, results, or interpretations.
3. Distinguish clearly between:
o what the paper directly reports,
o what the paper argues or defends,
o your own concise interpretation of the paper s contribution.
4. For important claims, findings, methods, datasets, and conclusions, include page numbers or section references where possible.
5. Preserve the abstract verbatim exactly as written in the paper.
6. When equations, statistical models, algorithms, or computational workflows are central to the paper, describe them explicitly.
7. When software, R/Python packages, databases, cohorts, omics platforms, or statistical tests are mentioned, extract them.
8. If the PDF contains tables or figures with important scientific content, summarize them separately.
9. If text extraction is uncertain because of OCR, formatting, scanned pages, missing characters, or unreadable tables, report this in the extraction notes.
10. Stay concise and laconic. Compact information is better than linguistic beauty and long phrasing
11. Output only Markdown.

Before finalizing, perform a consistency check:
* Does the title match the citation?
* Are the DOI and year consistent?
* Are all major methods mentioned in the Methods section captured in the methods inventory?
* Are all major results captured in the detailed summary?
* Are any claims unsupported by the PDF?
* Are missing values explicitly marked rather than guessed?
